Field glossary

Key water & ocean terms

Plain-language definitions for the science behind our field dispatches.

Aquifer

An underground layer of permeable rock, sand, or gravel that holds groundwater and supplies wells and springs.

Blue Carbon

Carbon captured and stored for centuries in coastal ecosystems — especially mangroves, seagrass meadows, and salt marshes.

Coral Bleaching

When heat or pollution stress causes corals to expel their symbiotic algae, turning skeletons white and risking starvation.

Dead Zone (Hypoxia)

Water so depleted of dissolved oxygen (< 2 mg/L) that fish and bottom-dwelling life cannot survive, often caused by nutrient runoff.

Eutrophication

Excess nitrogen and phosphorus washing into lakes or coasts, sparking dense algal blooms that block light and consume oxygen.

Ghost Gear

Abandoned, lost, or discarded fishing nets, lines, and traps that continue entangling marine wildlife for years.

Microplastics

Plastic fragments smaller than 5 mm — from broken-down bottles to synthetic laundry fibres and tyre dust.

Ocean Acidification

The drop in seawater pH as the ocean absorbs atmospheric CO₂, reducing carbonate ions needed by shellfish and corals.

Watershed

The entire land area where rain and snowmelt drain into a shared river, lake, wetland, or coastal bay.
